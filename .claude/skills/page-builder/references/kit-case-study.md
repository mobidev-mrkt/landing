# Case Study Kit

A case study is a sales asset, not a project diary. Rule of length: each section under 150 words, at
least one visual. It has to be scannable in 60 seconds. Two thousand words is not the goal; clarity is.

No number in the headline means the case is not ready. Go back to the delivery lead for the metric.

## Structure — 12 sections

**1. Title + headline metric** — `[what was done] + [the number]`. The number is mandatory.

**2. At-a-glance snapshot** — a scannable panel right under the title: industry, location, team size,
engagement model, duration, stack, headline result.

**3. About the client** — 2–3 sentences: business, industry, market position, headcount. Who they are
and why they should be taken seriously.

**4. The challenge** — split three ways: the **business** challenge (what outcome was needed and why
now), the **technical** challenge (what was hardest), the **delivery** challenge (timeline, team,
shifting requirements). Pull quote from the client about the pain sits here.

**5. Why us + approach** — why they chose you over the alternative, and how the work started:
discovery, sprints, milestones. Short timeline strip. This answers "why should we trust you" before
it becomes an objection.

**6. The solution — mapped 1:1 to the challenge** — every solution explicitly names the challenge it
closes. Business → …, technical → …, delivery → …. Features are woven in here, not listed separately.

**7. Results and business value** — before → after with numbers, in a table. Not "built a dashboard"
but "onboarding cut from four days to forty minutes". Second pull quote, about the result.

**8. Features delivered** — short list of output. Output is not outcome; do not merge this with results.

**9. Tech stack** — frontend, backend, infrastructure, integrations.

**10. Full testimonial** — 2–4 sentences plus name, role, photo or logo. The strongest one is about
ROI or trust.

**11. CTA** — "Facing something similar? →". A case study is a closer. Without an action the lead
evaporates.

**12. SEO / meta** — target query, meta title under 60 characters, description under 155, alt text on
visuals, internal links to adjacent cases and the relevant service page.

## Intake questionnaire — subject matter expert / client

One 45–60 minute interview should cover all twelve sections. Ask open questions and ask for numbers.

**A. Client context** — What does the company do, in which market, how many people? What is their
position in the niche? Who was the stakeholder on their side, by role? Engagement model, duration,
our team composition?

**B. Challenge** — What business result did they need and why then? What pressure existed — deadline,
investor, competitor? What was hardest technically: legacy, integrations, load, data? Delivery
challenges: timelines, team, unstable requirements, time zones? What would have happened if it had
not been solved — the cost of inaction? Had they tried before, with another vendor, and why did it fail?

**C. Why us and approach** — Why us over the competitor or in-house? Did it start with discovery, a
workshop, an audit? Key milestones and dates? What changed along the way?

**D. Solution** — How exactly was each of the three challenges solved? Which architectural or product
decisions were decisive, and why? What trade-offs were made deliberately? Which screen or flow best
illustrates the solution?

**E. Results — numbers required** — Headline metric and its figure? Before → after across three or
four metrics — time, money, conversion, users, growth? Business consequences: revenue, funding,
savings, speed, retention? What can the client now do that they could not before? Any data from three
or six months after launch?

**F. Output** — Which four to six key features or modules shipped? What does the client value most?

**G. Tech** — Full stack, third-party integrations, AI or data components? Why those technologies?

**H. Quote** — Will the client give an official quote with name, role and logo? One sentence on what
they would recommend you for? Can the company be named publicly, or must it be anonymised?

**I. Legal and media** — What cannot be published — figures, names, screens under NDA? Do they have
brand assets, team photos, product screenshots?

## Writer SOP

1. **Start from the number.** No figure in the headline, the case is not ready.
2. **One narrative, not a list.** Pain → why us → what we did → what came out. Each section sets up
   the next.
3. **Challenge ↔ solution 1:1.** Every solution names the challenge it closes.
4. **Outcome versus output.** Results are business consequences with before → after. Features are what
   shipped. Never merged.
5. **Active voice, past tense, specifics.** "Cut onboarding from four days to forty minutes", not
   "optimised processes".
6. **Run the humanisation pass** (`copy-humanisation.md`) as a separate step after the draft.
   Negative parallelism and punch triads are hard fails at the blind judge.
7. **Under 150 words per section.** If a section does not move the sale, delete it.
8. **At least two pull quotes** spread through the page — challenge and result — beyond the closing
   testimonial.
9. **CTA mandatory** at the end.
10. **Mark visuals in the draft:** `[HERO]`, `[SNAPSHOT]`, `[BEFORE/AFTER]`, `[DIAGRAM]`, `[SCREEN]`,
    `[METRIC]`, `[STACK]`, `[QUOTE]`.
11. **SEO pass:** query in H1 and the first 100 words, alt text, meta, internal links.
12. **Fact-check** every number and name against the SME answers before handover.

## Designer brief

| Marker | What to produce |
|---|---|
| `[HERO]` | Hero visual: the product in context plus the headline metric as a chip |
| `[SNAPSHOT]` | At-a-glance panel with icons: industry, team, duration, stack |
| `[BEFORE/AFTER]` | Before-and-after comparison, metrics or UI |
| `[DIAGRAM]` | Architecture, flow or process |
| `[SCREEN]` | Product screenshots in a device frame, clean, retina |
| `[METRIC]` | Large number chips for the results section |
| `[STACK]` | Technology logos in a row |
| `[QUOTE]` | Pull-quote card with client photo or logo |

Client's brand colours and type, white background behind screenshots. Real screens from the project,
never stock mockups; blur or anonymise anything under NDA. Contrast and readability on mobile, since
most traffic is a phone. Alt text on every visual. Export WebP, lazy-load, under 200KB where possible.

## Pre-launch checklist

- [ ] A number appears in the headline
- [ ] Snapshot panel filled
- [ ] Every challenge has a mapped solution
- [ ] Results in before → after form with figures
- [ ] Two or more pull quotes plus one full testimonial
- [ ] CTA present and clickable
- [ ] All `[markers]` replaced with final visuals
- [ ] Client legal approval on figures, name and screenshots — especially under NDA
- [ ] SEO: meta title and description, query, alt text, internal links
- [ ] Mobile check
