# Copy humanisation — the anti-AI-tell pass

Runs inside Phase 3, pass 2, after the copy is drafted and before anything else touches it. It is a
separate pass, not a mental note while writing. Writing and editing pull in opposite directions; a
drafter who is also policing their own tells does neither job well.

**If the `anticopywriting-ai` skill is installed, invoke it and let it run its own detect →
rewrite → self-check → final-pass loop. This file is the fallback and the scoring rubric.**

Why the pass exists: a page that reads as machine-written loses the reader before the offer lands.
The reader rarely thinks "this is AI". They think "this is a template" and close the tab. On a
service page that costs an enquiry; on a blog page it costs the citation, because AI search
increasingly favours pages with a discernible author behind them.

---

## The pass in four steps

1. **Read the draft aloud.** Anything you would not say to a prospect across a table gets marked.
2. **Sweep for the tells below**, category by category. Do not fix while sweeping; mark first.
3. **Rewrite the marked spans.** Replace, do not soften. A hedged version of a tell is still a tell.
4. **Ask once more: "what here still reads as machine-written?"** Give 2–4 honest answers and fix
   those. This second pass is what separates technically clean copy from copy with a person behind
   it.

---

## Tells to strip

### Content

- **Inflated significance.** "a key milestone", "a game-changing approach", "ushering in a new era".
  Nothing on a service page is a milestone. Say what it does.
- **Borrowed authority.** "leading publications note", "experts agree", "studies suggest" with no
  study named. Either name the source or cut the claim.
- **Empty participial tails.** "…, underscoring our commitment", "…, showcasing the breadth of our
  expertise", "…, highlighting the importance of". These clauses carry no information. Delete them
  whole; the sentence is finished without them.
- **Brochure adjectives.** "vibrant", "seamless", "robust", "cutting-edge", "world-class",
  "state-of-the-art", "bespoke". Replace with the specific fact that made you reach for the
  adjective, or cut.
- **The challenges-and-opportunities template.** "While challenges remain, the outlook is
  promising." Says nothing, appears everywhere.

### Language

- **Negative parallelism.** "not just X, but Y", "it isn't about X — it's about Y". The single most
  reliable AI signature in English marketing copy. Banned outright.
- **Rule-of-three punch triads.** "Faster. Cleaner. Cheaper." Break the rhythm: use two items, or
  four, or one long clause and one short.
- **Avoiding the plain verb.** "serves as", "represents", "constitutes", "stands as" where "is"
  would do.
- **False ranges.** "from strategy to execution", "from startups to enterprises". The two poles are
  chosen for rhythm, not meaning.
- **Elegant variation.** Calling the same thing "the platform", "the solution", "the offering" and
  "the system" across four paragraphs to avoid repeating a word. Repeat the word.
- **Latinate padding.** "utilise" for use, "leverage" for use, "facilitate" for help, "in order to"
  for to, "prior to" for before.

### Structure and formatting

- **Bold-headed vertical lists** where prose would carry the argument better. A list is for genuinely
  parallel items, not for making three sentences look organised.
- **Em-dash overuse.** More than one per paragraph reads as generated. Commas, full stops, and
  brackets exist.
- **Title Case In Every Heading.** Sentence case reads as written by a person.
- **Emoji in headings or list bullets** on a B2B service page.
- **Uniform paragraph length.** Three-sentence blocks all the way down is the clearest structural
  tell there is. Vary deliberately: a one-line paragraph, then a long one that takes its time.

### Voice

- **Assistant residue.** "I hope this helps", "Let's dive in", "In this article, we will explore",
  "Here's everything you need to know".
- **Hedging stacks.** "It could potentially be argued that in some cases…". Commit or cut.
- **Slogan endings.** A closing line that resolves everything neatly — "The future is bright", "One
  partner, endless possibilities". Real pages end on the next action, not on a chord.
- **No opinion anywhere.** A page that only states and never judges reads as compiled rather than
  written. Somewhere the business should say plainly what it thinks is the right way to do the work,
  and be willing to be disagreed with.

## Positive checks — copy with a person behind it

Stripping tells produces sterile copy, which is its own tell. The pass is only finished when these
are also true:

- [ ] **Specific over abstract.** "14 integrations migrated in six weeks, no downtime" beats
      "significant project experience".
- [ ] **Varied rhythm.** Short sentences next to long ones, on purpose.
- [ ] **The buyer's own words**, taken from calls, emails and replies — not the team's internal
      vocabulary.
- [ ] **Complexity admitted.** "This works well below 30 people and badly above it" is more credible
      than a page where everything is a fit.
- [ ] **A boundary stated.** What the service does *not* cover, said plainly. Nothing reads more
      human than a limit.
- [ ] **Numbers that are real**, and flagged as pending where they are not. Never invented to fill
      the slot.

---

## Scoring the axis

The **Human copy** axis is worth 10 points on both scorecards.

| Points | State |
|---|---|
| 9–10 | No banned tells. Rhythm varies. At least three positive checks clearly met. |
| 6–8 | One or two isolated tells, or the copy is clean but flat — no opinion, no boundary, uniform rhythm. |
| 3–5 | Tells recur across sections, or the page reads as a filled template. |
| 0–2 | Negative parallelism or punch triads present, brochure adjectives throughout. Return to the pass. |

If the `anticopywriting-ai` detector is available, its density figure gives a starting point:
under 5 markers per 1000 words is broadly clean, 5–15 needs work, above 15 fails the axis outright.
The detector is an input to the score, not the score itself — it cannot see whether anyone is home
in the writing.

**Score before fixing, not after.** Across many pages, the first number is what tells you whether
the problem is one writer or the whole template.
